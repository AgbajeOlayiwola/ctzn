# ctzn records policy

The record helps readers assess evidence. It does not rate politicians or recommend votes. Coverage currently follows the officials mapped for Shomolu, Lagos, including national officials. More entries do not imply better performance.

Apply these rules to every official:

- Include relevant, attributable background, public actions and significant accountability issues, whether favourable or unfavourable.
- Link each published item to a readable source. Identify the publisher and distinguish the event date from the publication date. The review date is not the event date.
- Label government, parliamentary and electoral material **Official record**. Government biographies are not independent credential audits.
- Label independently reported material **News report**, identifying the investigating organisation when it is not a newspaper. Reporting a commissioning is not ctzn's confirmation that a project is finished or functioning today.
- Label politicians' claims **Their own account**, including interviews, speeches and press releases copied into newspapers. A newspaper's masthead does not independently confirm the speaker's claims.
- Explain bill stages. Sponsorship, first reading, passage by one chamber and enactment are different. Do not claim delivered services from a bill's title or introduction.
- Attribute allegations and distinguish them from findings. Do not imply an official ordered an event merely because it occurred during their tenure.
- Show gaps: attendance, enacted legislation and independently inspected project delivery remain uncompiled unless supported. Missing entries are not evidence of inactivity.
- Keep user submissions separate and unreviewed. A contributor cannot promote a suggestion into the published record or a verified credential. In the local app these suggestions stay on the device.
- Correct errors throughout role listings, responsibility references and learning tasks. Preserve a short explanation of significant corrections. Do not rely on resident votes to establish an officeholder's identity.
